# M6 test publication contract

D-051 applies to the synthetic compiler family in this isolated crate. Each registry is configured for exactly one tenant and project through an explicit trusted constructor. The broker service has no registry dependency. The disposable profile assumes process and file ownership of its store directory. A privileged writer restoring the entire store to an old image is outside this test profile.

## Signed documents

The signature envelope has exactly `protected` and `signature`. The protected object and each purpose-specific payload are closed. The envelope version is `writ-detached-policy-signature/1` and the algorithm is `Ed25519`. The authorization purpose uses `writ-authorization-bundle/2.0`, the existing PolicyBundle with the legacy inline `publisher_signature` member absent. The acceptance purpose uses `writ-acceptance-policy/1`. Its full digest becomes the bundle SHA in the existing AcceptanceRequirements projection. The signed payload is the whole document in either case.

Parse JSON with the kernel's strict D-006 parser. Compute `payload_digest` as `sha256:` followed by the lowercase hexadecimal SHA-256 of the entire D-006 canonical UTF-8 payload. The signing input is the ASCII string `writ.policy.detached-signature/1`, one NUL byte, and the D-006 canonical UTF-8 encoding of the complete protected object. There is no trailing newline. Use pure Ed25519, with no prehash or context. Encode the 64-byte signature as 128 lowercase hexadecimal characters and enforce strict verification. The interval is inclusive at `not_before` and exclusive at `expires_at`, using canonical UTC seconds. Each key has its own independently enforced validity interval.

Resolve the named key solely from the configured verification source. Its record supplies the exact key ID, one purpose, one scope, a validity interval, revocation status and a 32-byte public key. Selection and consumption hold a current trust snapshot that orders revocation and human authorization changes against the whole operation. Requests and stored documents supply no root material. The signer receives exact protected bytes and is a separate interface. The fixture seed is confined to example and test code.

The [acceptance vector](contracts/signing-vector.json) and [authorization vector](contracts/authorization-signing-vector.json) use the RFC 8032 test key and an independent OpenSSL invocation. Each lists the exact signing bytes, detached signature and payload bytes. The vector key is shared only for interoperability examples. Each runtime trust record binds its key ID and public key to one purpose and scope. Metadata changes, cross-purpose and cross-scope substitution, changed content, invalid signatures and closed-object rejection are covered by executable tests.

## Publication and selection

Publication, selection and consumption rerun the existing compiler against the prerequisite draft. The signed bundle must equal the compiler output after removing its fixed null inline-signature placeholder. The `proof_digest` must equal the D-006 digest of the complete report’s `kernel_proof` member. The registry loads the bundle through the kernel and requires the laboratory's mandatory human review criterion in the acceptance projection. The fixture scope is confined to the synthetic action and the compiler’s proven tenant and project. Every statement must bind that sole tenant and use a resource prefix starting with the complete `synthetic:<tenant>/<project>/` segment. Kernel hard denials and the broker's exact decision and grant rules continue to apply.

The trusted publisher authority checks the exact canonical intent through fresh scoped human publisher authentication. It receives the purpose, operation, scope, expected activation revision, immutable publication version, full publication digest and the three individual content digests. The caller's presentation is opaque to the registry and cannot assert status. The authority returns canonical human and ceremony IDs, trusted issuance and exclusive expiry. The maximum ceremony life is 120 seconds. A committed audit records its single use. Human authority is rechecked at commit, then trusted time and both signatures are rechecked before committing. Past human provenance is audited history, while consumption and rollback check current policy, publisher keys, validity and retirement. Rollback needs a new authority presentation.

For each scope, `publish` appends the next immutable publication version and selects it. An existing exact payload pair cannot be republished, even with new signatures or reordered proof inputs. A previously selected authorization payload that differs from the current one requires rollback, even when combined with a fresh acceptance policy. The acceptance policy uses the immutable publication number as its canonical decimal policy version, limited to nine digits, and the fixed laboratory policy ID `m6-acceptance`. `rollback` explicitly selects an earlier immutable, unretired publication. `retire` permanently retires an inactive publication and appends audit without changing the activation revision. Publish and rollback append a higher activation revision and audit in one durable transaction. Version and revision are distinct. Durable selection reconstructs from the committed audit and immutable publication. Missing or inconsistent records refuse. All registry operations order before broker operations. A use spans validation, the broker's shared policy fence and effect completion. The example broker is bound to one registry. Permit presentation checks the ledger identity, scope, publication and carried activation revision before calling the broker, including after rollback to identical bundle bytes.

## Managed intents

D-055 adds the optional `management` object to `writ-human-policy-publisher/1`. It binds a context digest and expected human. Direct D-051 intents retain their original form. The managed registry entry point requires a host-configured witness for the active dispatch. The witness verifies the exact presentation, persisted management context and draft revision under the management draft fence. The registry also requires the existing publisher authority to return the same human, and rechecks both authorities and current policy before commit.

The registry commits the management digest with its own audit and includes it in reconstruction of the canonical intent fingerprint. Existing standalone stores open without migration. A first managed event adds its audit association table within the registry transaction. Readers use durable registry history to identify the receipt. A failed or ambiguous management-side request repeats no registry mutation during recovery.
